The only club where Palmer, Nicklaus and Player each built a nine

Twenty-seven private holes on 365 acres of Georgia pines and Savannah River bottomland in Evans, minutes from Augusta National.

An idea at the 1999 Champions Dinner

Once a year, on the Tuesday of Masters week, the only men at the table are those who have won a green jacket. At the 1999 dinner, Gary Player turned to Jack Nicklaus and Arnold Palmer with an idea: three champions, three courses, one property, just down the road from Augusta National.

Between them, 13 green jackets, 34 major championships and 159 professional victories.

Three index cards decided who built what

The land was 365 acres along the Savannah River: an island framed by two rivers, a bluff of rolling pines, a creek through wetlands and hardwoods. Player wrote Island, Bluff and Creek on three index cards. Palmer drew first, then Nicklaus. The last card belonged to the host. By 2005 all three nines were open.

The Palmer nine, on the Island

Palmer drew first, having played the most Masters.

“The Island Nine is flanked on one side by the broad Savannah River and on the other side by the Little River. … There is not a better setting for golf in the South.”

Arnold Palmer

The Nicklaus nine, on the Bluff

Nicklaus drew second, holding the most green jackets.

“Very little dirt has been moved, which means the course fits nicely with the existing terrain and its surroundings. This is the kind of site that provides you the opportunity to design a golf course that on opening day will appear as if it's been there for 50 years.”

Jack Nicklaus

The Player nine, on the Creek

The last card on the table belonged to the host.

“The opening four holes play a beautiful wetland area while the remaining holes move upland through native hardwood trees. The ninth dramatically plays over and around a large lake on the right.”

Gary Player

They play here first

Every spring since 2019, the Augusta National Women's Amateur has opened at Champions Retreat. The world's 72 finest women amateurs play the first two rounds on the Palmer and Nicklaus nines, and the top 30 advance to Augusta National.

A day at the club, by the clock

  1. 5:00 am

    The fitness center opens

    Upstairs in the Locker House, open every day until 11 at night. Below it: renovated locker rooms with rainfall showers.

  2. 7:00 am

    Breakfast in the Grille House

    Now with a large bar, refinished floors and a wine wall of over 200 bottles. The Lifestyle Shop opens at the same hour.

  3. An hour before your tee time

    Bag drop, then the range

    Leave your bag outside the Golf Shop and it will be waiting. Range balls are on the house.

  4. Your tee time

    A caddie on the bag

    Caddies are available for any round, and worth it the first few times out, given the course's blind shots and water carries.

  5. 11:00 am

    Lunch until five

    Southern flavors and wood-fired dishes, Tuesday to Saturday. Sunday is brunch until two.

  6. 4:00 pm

    Cottage check-in

    Each cottage takes four to eight couples. Check-out is at 11 the next morning.

  7. 5:00 pm

    Three Kings opens

    A hidden stairway leads to a members-only room named for Nicklaus, Palmer and Player. Wednesday to Saturday until 10.

Masters Week, the one week the gates open

Each April a limited number of Masters Week patrons play and stay as the club's guests. It is the only time Champions Retreat is open to the public.

  • A tee time package with unlimited buffet in the Barn until 4:00 pm, cooked by chefs from across the country
  • A $50 credit in the Golf Shop
  • Cottages for four to eight couples, with daily housekeeping, wine, beer and a bottle of spirits waiting

Coming as a guest

  1. At the gate

    Take the left lane at the community gate. Security may ask for your license and check the tee sheet.

  2. Parking

    Guest parking is in front of the Barn, on your left along Champions Parkway.

  3. Timing

    Arrive about an hour before your tee time, earlier if you're having lunch first.

  4. Dress

    Collared shirts tucked in and no denim on the course. Hats off indoors, soft spikes only.

  5. Phones

    On silent from arrival. Calls outdoors, and none in the Golf Shop, the dining room or on the range.
